Leia's lightfield display technology was groundbreaking, but their product ecosystem had grown inconsistently. Apps built in silos looked and behaved differently, creating friction for users and inefficiency for teams. I built the design system that unified their visual language and established reusable patterns across their suite of apps in time for the Lume Pad 2 launch.

Design Foundation

Built systematic approach to layering UI in 3D space. Working within an 8pt grid system, I defined depth zones, contrast requirements, and scale specifications for lightfield viewing. Extended Material Design's semantic variables with depth-specific adjustments. Typography established baseline grid alignment across all depth planes.

Component Architecture

Extended Material Design for Leia's Android platform with depth-specific adaptations. Buttons required higher contrast ratios because depth perception changes color relationships. Built navigation optimized for landscape tablets, modular media controls for 3D viewing, and extended Material icon sets.

Each component included technical implementation guidelines—not just visual specs, but the reasoning behind depth placement and contrast decisions so engineers understood why components behaved differently in 3D space.

Brand Systems

Led brand refresh parallel to design system work. Logo refinements improved optical balance, custom product wordmarks established consistent proportions, and consolidated color palette used semantic variables connecting to Material Design's layering system. Brand tokens became the foundation for color, typography, and visual hierarchy across Leia's product ecosystem.

Adoption Strategy

Drove systematic rollout through phased app-by-app implementation. 1:1 working sessions ensured designers understood not just what to build, but why depth and contrast behaved differently. Collaborated directly with engineering teams to establish design-code parity—Figma components mapped directly to Android implementations. Weekly stakeholder presentations maintained alignment across product, engineering, and leadership.

Within six months: Cross-functional adoption achieved, design-to-development time reduced by estimated 75% as teams stopped rebuilding components from scratch.
